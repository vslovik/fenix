Early stage suits me. Founding-engineer shape at a seed-stage startup: owning the architecture
from zero, shipping the first production system, growing the team that carries it, and defining
scope alongside the founders rather than inheriting one set elsewhere. Salary plus equity. I
work well solo and in small, transparent teams with real technical ownership.

I want to build and operate agentic AI systems in production. The work that fits me best sits
where software architecture, LLM-based agents, and production reliability meet: designing how
agents plan, call tools, retrieve context and recover from failure, then being the person who
catches it when they don't and redesigns the system so it doesn't happen again. I want the
technical-leadership and architecture side of that role. Retrieval is part of that and I work
on it directly: embeddings, vector search, chunking strategy, ranking quality, and answers
grounded in the sources they came from, with citations back to the chunk that carried them.

I treat evaluation and observability as the engineering rather than the write-up. Labelled
evaluation sets with a deliberate control group of things that should score badly. Blinded
human rating, with the scores hidden and the order shuffled, so the judgement stays
independent of the ranking it is judging. Precision at k. Regression tests that pin a result
so a defect cannot come back quietly. Traces, latency and quality drift visible in production.
I want to own that layer — the measurement that says whether a system is getting better or
only getting bigger.

Behind that, 15+ years in production systems, and hands-on NLP and Transformer depth used
through an engineering lens: fine-tuning and shipping models into systems that have to stay
up. I found a validation defect in my own pipeline, quantified what it changed, and restated
every figure it had touched. That discipline — what separates a model that scores well from a
model that holds up — is what I bring to the agent side.
